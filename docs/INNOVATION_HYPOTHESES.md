# Innovation Hypotheses (Phase 2) — each falsifiable, with a pre-registered success criterion

Written BEFORE any experiment was run (2026-10-18); reconciled the same day with the prior-art search (`research/PRIOR_ART_AND_DIFFERENTIATION.md`). Status is updated only by `docs/EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md`.

## What the prior-art search changed
- **Commonplace in 2026 public C-MAPSS repos (cannot be claimed as differentiators):** conformal/CQR intervals, Evidently-style drift monitoring, MLflow tracking and registry, engine-level splits, PHM08 reporting, Streamlit dashboards, drift-triggered retraining without a gate, per-prediction JSONL logs, single-threshold cost policies with failures-per-100-engines, alert precision/recall with lead time.
- **Closest prior item:** sik04 "IntelliTwin" (MIT, pushed 2026-10-17): replay simulator + conformal calibration + cost scenarios + four control policies on FD001/FD002/FD004, results not started; no agent, no gates, no stress tests, no dashboard. Cited in every deck and README where H1 appears.
- **Peer-reviewed precedent for decision metrics:** Lee & Mitici (RESS 2023: unscheduled-maintenance rate, wasted life), de Pater et al. (RESS 2022: alarm + safety factor), Mitici et al. (RESS 2023: lower-bound trigger), Kamariotis et al. (RESS 2024: cost per unit time). None releases a pipeline.
- **Not found anywhere:** measured abstention/escalation applied to RUL work orders; a promotion gate that uses a maintenance-decision metric and demonstrates a reject on C-MAPSS; injected-input stress tests scored on the agent's silent-wrong-order rate; work orders recording rules fired and model alias; a decision-stability (churn) metric.
- **Consequence:** the differentiation claim is re-weighted toward H2, H3, H4 and H6; H1 is kept as the evaluation backbone with prior art cited; nothing is called "first" or "novel".

Common protocol (applies to all): official train/test split respected; all preprocessing fit on training engines only; engine-level splits; thresholds tuned on tuning engines never used for evaluation; 5 seeds where randomness exists; subsets FD001–FD004; results reported per subset, never pooled to hide a failure.

What would prove the main idea unsuccessful: H2 failing (abstention does not remove errors) together with H3 failing (the agent still issues confident orders on corrupted inputs). In that case the submission still has a complete, audited, gated pipeline and reports the negative result honestly; the differentiator becomes the decision-level evaluation harness and the visible gate behaviour.

Assumptions exposed: cost-sensitive variants (policy D) require an assumed cost ratio of unplanned failure to early replacement; experiments sweep the ratio (5×–50×) and never state a real-world saving.
