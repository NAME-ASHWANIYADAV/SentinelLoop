# Demo Script (Phase 7, final) — about 7 minutes in seven beats, every number from `docs/NUMBERS.md`

Numbers come from the 2026-10-18 evidence run (`artifacts/<subset>/metrics.json`, `experiments/results/<exp>/summary.json`). Say "simulated" whenever C-MAPSS or an injected fault is on screen. Commands and reset procedure: `docs/DEMO_RUNBOOK.md`. Beats 3 and 5 are live commands; everything else reads shipped artefacts.

## Beat 1 — The operational problem (40 s)
"A fleet of machines streams sensor data. Today failures are caught after a breakdown or by fixed service intervals. The case study asks for a closed loop: ingest, detect degradation, predict remaining useful life, raise a work order automatically, and involve a human only for exceptions." Show the architecture slide (data → clean → features → models → Triage Agent → work order, with the drift → retrain → gate → registry side loop).

## Beat 2 — Why a point estimate and a threshold are not enough (50 s)
"A point RUL estimate with a threshold answers neither of the two questions a maintenance manager asks: how sure is this estimate, and can the inputs be trusted. We add a calibrated 90% interval and quality and drift monitors, and we evaluate the agent as a maintenance policy, not by RMSE alone." Show the leakage slide: the naive validation protocol reports RMSE 2.5–6.9; the honest protocol 12.4–16.6; we report the honest number and both RMSEs, capped and raw [E0].

## Beat 5 — Retraining on new data, live, then the measured evidence (120 s)
Terminal: `python -m sentinelloop retrain --subset FD003 --simulate-new-data --fast --out-dir artifacts_demo` (about 20 s). "Thirty engines the champion has never seen arrive as a file; a challenger is trained on the champion's data plus the new engines and gated on held-out validation engines: RMSE within 2%, coverage at least 0.85, late-order rate not worse. The gate prints its decision and its reasons either way." Read the printed decision.
Experiments tab (filtered to the subset):
- **Gated retraining (E4)**: "In the stored scenarios the more-data challenger is promoted on three subsets, a challenger trained on wrongly labelled data is rejected on all four with the reasons logged, and the drift monitor fires on a regime shift and a sensor bias while staying silent on 20 clean windows. Two honest caveats: a harmless sensor offset was promoted on two subsets because it did not hurt validation metrics, and our absolute coverage floor rejected FD001's better challenger; the floor should be relative to the champion."
- **Policy replay (E1)**: "We scored the agent as a maintenance policy by replaying complete engines. At the generic 25-cycle threshold neither the point policy nor the lower-bound policy is late on three of four subsets, and the lower-bound policy discards 11 to 15 more cycles of life per engine. At matched late-rate targets the two trade late orders against discarded life, and tuned lower-bound thresholds are unstable across seeds. We report that trade-off; the interval is a conservative operating point the operator chooses, not a free improvement." Point at the C_lower_T vs A_point_T rows and the frontier table; the caption says NOT SUPPORTED for H1 and H2 and we say so first.
- **Decision metrics vs RMSE (E5)**: "RMSE orders our point models within one to two cycles of discarded life, and it cannot see the policy at all: the same model acted on through its lower bound has the same RMSE and 11 to 15 cycles more discarded life. The agent is accepted on the replay, not on RMSE."
- **Stress tests (E3)**: "We corrupted half the test engines: missing values, 3-sigma noise, a shifted operating regime, a biased sensor. The quality and drift monitors flagged 98 to 100% of corrupted engines for three of the four faults with zero additional false alarms per engine. The point-threshold agent issued silent wrong orders on 0 to 20% of corrupted engines; the full agent on none, mostly because a fleet-level drift alarm escalates every serious decision while it is active; per-engine evidence alone leaves 0 to 12%, and the fleet rule escalates 25 to 36% of healthy engines while the alarm is on. A single biased sensor is caught only at fleet level on the single-regime subsets: that is our documented detection floor."

## Beat 6 — What happens when data quality or uncertainty becomes unacceptable (40 s)
Model & monitoring tab: the drift report, the quality block and the decision-vs-truth table. "When imputed inputs exceed 10% of the window, when a setting leaves its training range, when the fleet drifts, or when the interval is wider than the calibrated limit, the agent escalates: an INSPECTION order for a human instead of a confident maintenance order. On clean data that happens for 8 to 19% of engines; on corrupted inputs for 32 to 100% depending on the fault." Audit tab: click verify → chain valid, 0 mismatches on this subset's log [E6: 3,409 decisions across the four subsets, 0 mismatches, tampering detected].

## Beat 7 — Limitations and when not to trust it (40 s)
"C-MAPSS is simulated. Interval coverage is marginal and subset-dependent: 0.94 on FD001, 0.85 on FD004, below nominal and reported. The official test set was used for three design choices. Width-based abstention alone did not reduce decision errors on clean data. Thresholds must be tuned per fleet on many engines; ours are unstable on thirty. The audit chain detects in-place edits, not an insider rewriting it. No CMMS integration. Every one of these is in the known-limitations document and on the limitations slide."
