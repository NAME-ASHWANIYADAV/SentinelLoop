# Reliability and safety

Status 2026-10-18, after the Phase 9 adversarial review (`ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md`), its fixes, the re-review of those fixes (PD-28; `ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md`, "Re-review of the fixes") and the final handoff-audit fixes (`tests/test_app_handoff_fixes.py`). This document covers how SentinelLoop behaves when its inputs, models or own components fail, how its states change, how its stated uncertainty compares with outcomes (calibration, §4), and how to recover. **All evidence is on SIMULATED (C-MAPSS) or SYNTHETIC (wind) data, plus software tests. Nothing here is field-validated** (`VALIDATION_LADDER.md`).

## 1. Safety position

1. **Advisory only.** SentinelLoop has no connection to control systems. It cannot start, stop or reconfigure equipment, and it never executes maintenance. Every recommendation becomes a work order that a named person approves, defers or rejects (principle 8).
2. **Not a safety function.** SentinelLoop is not part of any safety function or safety instrumented function. It claims no SIL, takes no credit as a protection layer, and must not be counted as a barrier or safeguard in any hazard analysis (HAZOP, LOPA, bow-tie or similar). It has not been assessed or certified against IEC 61508, IEC 61511 or IEC 62443 (wording from `research/R5_integration_standards.md`; finding safety-12). Turbine protection systems, alarm handling and the operator's procedures stay in charge, and plant safety must be complete without SentinelLoop. The console's sign-in page says "Not part of any safety function" (`src/sentinelloop/console/app.py`).
3. **Fail visible, not silent.** When data, scope or the model fail, the asset shows *why* there is no assessment: DATA_QUALITY_FAILURE, MODEL_OUT_OF_SCOPE or MODEL_UNAVAILABLE. These states are never shown as healthy, and they never produce a number. An asset that was never assessed shows NOT_ASSESSED, and one whose assessments have stopped shows ASSESSMENT_OVERDUE (§2).
4. **Warnings do not disappear by themselves.** URGENT_REVIEW and INSPECTION_RECOMMENDED latch. They clear only when a person completes a work order for the warning (raised after it began, or an older order that the decision engine linked to it and sent back for approval) whose outcome records an inspection of the equipment (the platform cannot check that the inspection took place), or when a planner, reliability engineer or admin dismisses them with a written reason (PD-16, refining PD-08; PD-28). A later benign reading, bad data, a model outage or a back-dated assessment does not clear them. A back-dated assessment can raise a latch, never lower it (§2).
5. **No invented numbers.** Anomaly-only models (wind) produce no failure probability or remaining life. Decisions then use stated policy defaults (PD-11). The RUL model's P(failure) is not calibrated (§4). It is labelled "model-implied ... not calibrated" everywhere the platform renders it as text: the console's explanation line on work-order cards, the assessment reasons, the decision "why" text (including both safety-limit lines), and the CMMS request description's P(failure) and "Why:" lines (§4; `tests/test_app_handoff_fixes.py`).

## 2. States and transitions

**Definitions:** `src/sentinelloop/app/states.py`, module docstring.

**Classification precedence for one assessment:**
1. Model unavailable.
2. Data-quality failure.
3. Out of scope.
4. Risk (or anomaly).

**Labels** shown with or instead of the state (`AssetState.labels`, `health.asset_view`):
- NOT_ASSESSED: no assessment has been applied yet. Never shown as NORMAL_MONITORING.
- AWAITING_ASSESSMENT: a latch was cleared; shown until the next applied assessment.
- INSPECTION_PENDING: a latched inspection is hidden behind a data, scope or model state.
- ASSESSMENT_OVERDUE: the last applied assessment is older than `max_assessment_age_hours` (6 h by default, 26 h for wind; `configs/platform.yaml`), or the state shows a result but no time of assessment is on record (for example a state row restored without its assessments; `health.asset_view`).
- HUMAN_APPROVAL_REQUIRED: an order for the asset waits for approval.

**Latching and clearing (PD-16, refined by PD-28).**
- An URGENT or INSPECTION assessment latches the asset and records the wall-clock time the latch began. An escalation from INSPECTION to URGENT restarts the latch (`states.advance`; code only: no test asserts the new start on this path), so an order raised for the earlier inspection does not count for the urgent latch unless the decision engine links it (next bullet).
- **Which open orders count for a latch** (`decision._decide`, `workorders.addresses_latch`; re-review decision-lifecycle-01, latching-states-04):
  - A data or review job (`instrument_check`, `desk_review`, or the site's configured `data_job` and `review_job`) never stands in for an inspection or urgent job: the needed order is raised as well. An open equipment order (not a DRAFT) covers a desk-review recommendation, since a person is already going to look at the asset.
  - An order raised before the latch began is **linked** to it only if its job is the recommended, inspection or urgent job and it is PENDING_APPROVAL or DEFERRED. The engine then escalates it in place (its class and due date only move earlier) and it goes back for approval for its new purpose. That system escalation, recorded after the latch began, is what lets its completion clear the latch. A re-prioritisation by a person does not link.
  - An older order for one of those jobs that is approved, scheduled or in progress is **superseded**: the latch gets a new order whose explanation names the older one. A routine job (for example a lubrication) never counts for a latch: it is left as it was, and the latch gets its own order, whose explanation does not name it (`test_a_routine_order_approved_before_the_latch_never_stands_in_for_it`). A DRAFT never stands in.
- A latch is cleared in exactly two ways:
  1. **Completing a work order** that meets all four conditions (`workorders._latch_keep_reasons`):
     - it was raised after the latch began, or linked to it as above;
     - its job addresses the equipment. Not `instrument_check` or `desk_review`, nor the site's configured `data_job` or `review_job`, nor any data or review job recorded in the policy of a system order in this database (`workorders.recorded_non_clearing_jobs`). The API complete route passes the served decision config's names (`decision.non_clearing_jobs`; decision-lifecycle-02);
     - its outcome records an inspection: `degradation_found`, `failure_found` or `no_fault_found` (not `not_inspected` or `other`);
     - it was completed by a person (technician, planner or admin), not by the `integration` machine role. `HUMAN_CLEARERS` also names the reliability engineer, but that role cannot complete orders (`workorders.TRANSITIONS["complete"]`).
  2. **A dismissal with a written reason**: `POST /assets/{id}/clear-risk` with `event: dismissed`, by a planner, reliability engineer or admin. `clear-risk` refuses `maintenance_completed` (422) and refuses technicians (403) (`api.py`, `clear_risk`). Since the final handoff audit the service behind it, `health.clear_latched_risk`, also refuses any event but `dismissed`, for every role, so a script or new route that calls the service cannot record maintenance either (`tests/test_app_handoff_fixes.py`: `test_the_service_layer_refuses_a_free_standing_maintenance_completed`). The state-machine function `states.clear_risk`, which `workorders.complete` calls, still accepts `maintenance_completed` from technicians, planners, reliability engineers and admins (`states.CLEAR_EVENTS`). It holds no database connection: code that used it outside a completion would have to write the state row itself (`THREAT_MODEL.md` T5, T7).
- A latch whose start is unknown fails closed: no completion clears it, only a reasoned dismissal. Because no order could clear it, the decision engine adds none beside an open one: any open order (not a DRAFT) for the recommended, inspection or urgent job stands in for it, whatever its state and however old, and may be escalated in place. Only an order in progress at a lower class gets a separate, more urgent order beside it; with no such order open, one is raised as usual (`decision._decide`, docstring; code only, no test). Migration 0007 backfills the start of latches that existed before migration 0006 with the time their state row was last written, which is at or after the true start. The kept-latch reason therefore says the order was "raised before the recorded start of the latched condition" and that on an upgraded database that start may be an upper bound (wind-eval-migrations-09).
- A completion that fails any condition keeps the latch. The outcome gets the flag `latch_kept`, the asset gets a note with the reasons, and the asset's own audit history records `asset_state.latch_kept` (a clearing completion records `asset_state.maintenance_completed` there; api-security-console-5).
- After either kind of clearing the asset shows AWAITING_ASSESSMENT, never NORMAL_MONITORING, until it is assessed again.
- **Replays raise, never lower** (PD-28; latching-states-01). An assessment for an instant at or before the last applied one is a replay. It never moves the live state, with one exception: an URGENT_REVIEW or INSPECTION_RECOMMENDED replay above the current latch raises the latch (`states.escalate`). The latch start is the wall-clock time. The last health, the data-quality streak and the last applied instant stay as they were. The replay's reasons say so, the audit log records `asset_state.escalated_by_replay`, and `assess-range` reports `replays_escalated`. A replay raises nothing when an earlier assessment for the same instant already had that finding, because a person has cleared it since: on an export-fed site the scheduled job assesses the same latest reading until the next export, and must not undo a dismissal or a completed inspection. The order raised for a replay-raised latch cites the latest applied assessment, not the replay (KNOWN_LIMITATIONS M10).
- **Assessment instants** (PD-17, PD-28). An instant more than 5 min in the future, before 2000-01-01, or (for an asset with readings) before its first reading minus 5 min is refused (`health._check_instants`); `assess-range` checks every instant before it applies any, so a refused range stores nothing. Migration 0008 backfills the last applied instant and time on databases upgraded from before migration 0006, which had lost the replay protection (latching-states-02; §3 F23).

**Work-order rules that bear on safety** (PD-18, PD-28; tests in `tests/test_app_remediation.py` unless stated; RR = `tests/test_app_rereview_decision.py`):
- Work orders have 10 states, and every transition is role-checked (`workorders.TRANSITIONS`; `tests/test_app_workorders.py`).
- An urgent condition that arises while lower-priority work is in progress raises its own P1 order (`test_an_urgent_condition_during_lower_priority_work_raises_its_own_p1`). Work in progress whose recommendation only moves its due date earlier is left alone, not an error (RR: `test_work_in_progress_with_an_earlier_due_date_is_unchanged_not_an_error`).
- A deferred order returns to PENDING_APPROVAL once its date has passed, the next time `POST /decisions/generate` runs (`workorders.reactivate_due`; `test_a_deferred_order_returns_for_approval_when_its_date_passes`). A failing reactivation is reported for its order and the rest of the fleet run goes on (RR: `test_one_failing_reactivation_does_not_abort_the_fleet_run`). A deferred order comes back for approval at once, under the role that re-prioritised it, when a person or the engine sets it to P1, or when the engine raises its class (P3 to P2 included) or links it to a new latch (`workorders.reprioritise`, `bring_back`; decision-lifecycle-10). Tested for a person's escalation to P1 and for an engine escalation that both raises the class and links (RR: `test_a_manual_reprioritisation_keeps_the_score_and_records_only_what_changed`, `test_a_deferred_order_escalated_by_a_new_latch_comes_back_for_approval`); a rise without a link, and a link without a rise: code only. The length of a P1 deferral is not capped (KNOWN_LIMITATIONS M9).
- A reasoned rejection holds back the same condition for 24 h, 168 h or 720 h (P1, P2, P3 by default; `DecisionConfig.reject_suppress_h`), on every path that creates an order, including the separate P1 during work in progress. While a latch is active the condition is the latch episode, whatever state is displayed: any rejected order, of any origin, for the recommended, inspection or urgent job that belonged to the current latch suppresses it. A new latch episode, after a dismissal or a completed inspection, is raised again. When the latch start is unknown, the rejection holds until a dismissal or a completed-maintenance audit event follows it. An escalation is raised at once (`test_a_reasoned_rejection_is_not_re_raised_for_the_same_condition`; RR: `test_a_rejected_separate_p1_during_work_in_progress_stays_suppressed`, `test_a_rejection_holds_for_the_latched_condition_whatever_the_displayed_state`, `test_a_rejection_does_not_suppress_a_new_latch_episode`, `test_a_rejected_stand_in_holds_for_the_latched_condition`, `test_a_rejected_order_the_engine_brought_back_holds_for_the_latched_condition`, `test_a_rejection_still_suppresses_a_latch_whose_start_is_unknown`).
- Re-prioritisation: a system escalation keeps the earlier due date; a manual re-prioritisation keeps the risk score and records only the fields that changed; `POST /work-orders/{id}/reprioritise` accepts `expected_version` and answers 409 to a stale one (RR: `test_an_escalation_never_postpones_an_earlier_due_date`, `test_a_manual_reprioritisation_keeps_the_score_and_records_only_what_changed`, `test_reprioritise_can_refuse_a_stale_version`; `tests/test_app_rereview_wiring.py`: `test_the_reprioritise_route_refuses_a_stale_version`).
- `hand_back` returns a scheduled or started job to APPROVED, with a reason, and clears its schedule (`test_a_technician_can_hand_back_a_job_with_a_reason`).
- An out-of-scope or unavailable criticality-A turbine gets a `desk_review`, not a climb (`test_out_of_scope_criticality_a_turbines_get_a_desk_review_not_a_climb`). A desk review never clears a latch and never stands in for an urgent job (RR: `test_a_desk_review_never_stands_in_for_an_urgent_job`).
- Wind urgent orders say "a policy threshold, not a failure-time prediction" and make no failure-time claim (`test_anomaly_urgent_wording_makes_no_failure_time_claim`).
- A site decision config is refused when loaded if it lacks any of the P1, P2 and P3 keys, holds a value that is not a finite number (booleans, strings and null included), a probability outside [0, 1), a due date outside (0, 8760] h or a suppression period outside [0, 8760] h, or names an urgent or inspection job that is a data or review job. Before the re-review fixes such files were accepted: `p_safety` above 1, NaN or `true` silently disabled the safety-impact P1 rule, and a missing P-key made every recommendation without a prediction fail with no order (decision-lifecycle-02, -04, -05). `admin serve` also refuses a config whose `inspection_job` a registered asset type lacks (RR: `test_unsafe_decision_config_values_are_refused_at_load`, `test_job_names_that_would_turn_an_equipment_job_into_a_check_are_refused`; `tests/test_app_rereview_wiring.py`: `test_serve_refuses_a_decision_config_naming_a_job_the_sites_asset_types_lack`).

## 3. Failure modes and responses

Each evidence entry names the test that exercises the behaviour. Where a test covers only part of a row, the row says so (finding safety-8). Four of the tests cited below are in `tests/test_app_health.py`, which runs on the NASA C-MAPSS files in the git-ignored `data/raw/` (`python -m sentinelloop data --download`) and is skipped in a clone without them: `test_missing_signal_stale_data_and_stuck_sensor_are_data_quality_failures` (F1, F2), `test_conflicting_redundant_sensors_are_a_data_quality_failure` (F4), `test_an_engine_from_another_operating_envelope_is_out_of_scope` (F7) and `test_tampered_model_artifact_makes_the_model_unavailable` (F10).

## 4. Calibration (RUL model; SIMULATED)

**C1 interval audit** (`product/evidence/calibration/c1.md`, out-of-fold predictions on C-MAPSS FD001–FD004):
- Overall coverage of the nominal 90% interval is 0.904–0.931.
- **Coverage is not uniform over life.**
  - In the healthy band (true RUL 100–125, most rows) it is 0.94–0.97.
  - In the decision-relevant band (true RUL 25–50) it is **0.81–0.87 in every subset**.
  - In the last 25 cycles it is 0.86–0.94.
- Coverage across the six operating regimes of FD002/FD004 is even (0.91–0.92). The weakness lies in the life stage, not in the regime.

**Alternative methods** (engine-level split; descriptive, since rows within an engine are dependent):
- A global conformal re-correction narrows the intervals and lowers late-life coverage.
- Mondrian re-correction by predicted-RUL band raises 25–50 coverage in three of four subsets (FD001 0.814 → 0.874, FD004 0.829 → 0.865, FD002 0.833 → 0.840; FD003 unchanged at 0.869), at width changes of −1.6 to +1.3 cycles.
- **No method tested reaches 90% in the late-life bands.**

**C2 probability audit** (`product/evidence/calibration/c2.md`; P(failure before 60 h) read from the same out-of-fold intervals, 3 h per cycle):
- **P(failure) is not calibrated.** In the most populated high bin (shown 0.50–0.70), the mean shown is 0.54–0.57 while 0.89–0.94 of those rows fail within 60 h, in all four subsets.
- **The due-date rule fails more often than it states.** The latest-start rule targets P(failure before the job ends) ≤ 0.05. In the last 50 cycles of life (true RUL 0–50) the true failure came first in 9–18% of rows (FD001 0.148 and 0.184; FD004 0.160 and 0.171), and in 9.5–15.8% at true RUL 50–100.
- Isotonic recalibration on an engine split lowers the Brier score by 37–50% (FD001 0.0287 → 0.0158, FD002 0.0286 → 0.0163, FD003 0.0220 → 0.0110, FD004 0.0271 → 0.0171). It is **not shipped**: it needs a held-out calibration set from the customer's assets (PD-19). OPEN.
- The number is labelled everywhere the platform renders it as text (TECHNICAL_DEBT F1):
  - the console's explanation line on work-order cards: "Model-implied P(failure ...) (not calibrated ...)" (`console/client.py`, `explanation_lines`);
  - the assessment reasons: "P(failure before horizon) 0.xx (model-implied, not calibrated) >= urgent threshold ..." and the same for the inspection threshold (`states.classify`), which the console's Asset page and the cards' "Assessment reasons" line show (`test_assessment_reasons_label_the_failure_probability_as_not_calibrated`);
  - the decision "why" text for safety-impact assets, in both safety-limit lines: "P(failure) 0.xx (model-implied, not calibrated) >= safety limit ..." (`decision._plan_one`, called by `decision.recommend`), which the cards' "Why" line shows (`test_the_safety_limit_reason_labels_the_failure_probability_as_not_calibrated`, both lines);
  - the CMMS request description, which field crews read: its P(failure) line, "P(failure before <horizon> h): 0.xx (model-implied, not calibrated; see SentinelLoop KNOWN_LIMITATIONS E6)" (`integration.odoo_payload`; `test_the_cmms_description_labels_the_failure_probability_as_not_calibrated`), and its "Why:" line, which carries the labelled why text. The shipped safety limit `p_safety` 0.05 (`decision.DecisionConfig`, `configs/decision.example.yaml`) is below `p_inspect` 0.10, so every INSPECTION_RECOMMENDED order on a safety-impact RUL asset sends the number to the CMMS in that line, now with the label.
- The API's raw JSON fields (e.g. `explanation.p_fail_decision_horizon`) are numbers without text. The label states the problem; it does not fix it: the number shown is still not calibrated.
- The wind vertical shows no probability at all (PD-11).

**Consequences:**
1. The intervals are described by their measured coverage, never as calibrated without this qualifier and never as a conditional guarantee near failure: coverage is marginal, near nominal over out-of-fold data (0.904–0.931, C1), below nominal on the FD004 test set (0.85 against 0.90) and against the raw, uncapped truth (0.58–0.87), and 0.81–0.87 in late life (`docs/NUMBERS.md`; `CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md` P2, which stays NOT_SUPPORTED).
2. **The platform ships the probabilistic trigger for RUL models, not a threshold on the point prediction** (finding data_scientist-3). The state comes from P(failure before the decision horizon), read from the interval (`health.assess`, `risk.summarise`), against `p_inspect` 0.10 and `p_urgent` 0.30 with a 60 h horizon for C-MAPSS (`states.classify`, `configs/platform.yaml`). These are the untuned S1 settings; tuning in the base scenario chose 0.05 and 0.20 instead, in both S1d and S2. In S2 (SIMULATED; `SCHEDULING_EVALUATION.md` conclusion 7 and §4.3) the shipped settings come within 2 units of base-tuned RISK_GREEDY in the `base`, `tight_labour` and `parts_tight` scenarios, but cost 20.3 more under degraded (biased) predictions (worse on 8/8 seeds) and 8.4 more under approval delay (7/8). The trigger and the due date depend on exactly the calibration that C1 and C2 show is weakest in late life. A per-site tuned point-threshold trigger is PLANNED (PD-27; KNOWN_LIMITATIONS M7). This path runs only on SIMULATED C-MAPSS data; no pilot depends on it.
3. Mondrian recalibration of the intervals by predicted band, and isotonic recalibration of P(failure), are the upgrades to make if an RUL model family is put into use (PLANNED; neither is in the bundle).

**Stress evidence carried over from the competition** (INJECTED faults on C-MAPSS): detection of missing values, noise and regime shift at 98–100% with zero per-engine false alarms, with a single-sensor bias as the detection floor on two subsets (`experiments/results/e3_stress/`, claim P4).

## 6. Residual risks (also in `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md`)

- Real-data detection performance is unknown until G0 (rung 3) and a customer back-test (rung 4).
- The wind alarm setting was chosen on synthetic data only. The shipped setting (margin 1.25, sustain 18 operating points, chosen by W3's pre-stated rule, PD-15) gave 1 healthy alarm episode in 2.27 turbine-years on fresh held-out SYNTHETIC fleets (0.44 per turbine-year; 0.61 per assessable turbine-year). The 95% interval runs up to about 2.45, so the proposed pilot ceiling of 2 is not excluded. The rate is sensitive to persistence: an earlier run's setting (W2 run 2, sustain 6) gave 5.74 healthy alarm episodes per turbine-year on its own held-out fleets 20–25 (`w2.md`). The generator shares the model's assumptions (KNOWN_LIMITATIONS E3), so the real rate is unknown until G0. The shipped setting's figures are in `evidence/wind_synthetic/w3.md`; the settings the W2 runs chose (sustain 12 in `w2_run1.md`, sustain 6 in `w2.md`) are no longer shipped, so where another document quotes a different figure for the shipped setting, w3.md holds (the 95% interval: PD-15, `evidence/g0/PREREGISTRATION.md` §11.2; the risk itself: KNOWN_LIMITATIONS E7).
- Slow sensor drift raises false inspections (F18).
- A planner can defer a P1 order to any date (M9). An order raised for a latch that a replay raised cites the latest applied assessment, not the replay (M10). The wind rotor/generator check covers the last scoring window only (M11).
- A CMMS create that commits after the follow-up marker search can leave a duplicate request, dead-lettered for a person (S10).
- Commissioning on a period that already contains a developing fault hides that fault (PD-12).
- RUL intervals under-cover in late life, and the RUL model's P(failure) is not calibrated (§4).
- Direct edits of state tables in the database file are not detected by the audit chain (PD-22).
- The in-process rate limit and the single SQLite file suit one site (load test L1).
