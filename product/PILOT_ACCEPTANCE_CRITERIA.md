# Pilot acceptance criteria

> **NOT YET FIELD VALIDATED.** These are proposed criteria. Every number marked *(to agree)* is a starting proposal, to be fixed with the partner **before** the back-test or pilot data are seen. They are recorded in writing with the date. Criteria agreed after seeing results do not count.

## 1. Principles

- **Fixed in advance.** Criteria are set before the evidence is seen.
- **Several criteria, all of which must pass.** A pass on detection with an unbearable alarm burden is a fail.
- **Small numbers stay uncertain.** Event counts in a 12-week pilot will be small. Every rate is reported with a Wilson 95% interval, and no precision claim is made from a handful of events.
- **Business value is not a criterion here.** Rung 5 is a read-only shadow pilot, so it cannot show any downtime, cost or availability benefit. Those belong to a rung-6 trial in which recommendations are acted on, with a baseline agreed beforehand (§4).

**Note on B3: unit and evidence.**
- **Unit.** W2 and W3 count healthy alarm episodes per turbine-year: an episode is a run of consecutive sustained exceedances (`nbm._episodes`). The platform raises orders per latch, not per episode.
  - The first sustained alarm latches the turbine at INSPECTION_RECOMMENDED until a qualifying inspection is completed or the latch is dismissed (PD-16, `states.advance`). Later episodes during the latch do not start a new one.
  - While an open order stands in for the recommendation, a new recommendation escalates it in place or is absorbed by it (`decision._decide`). Only an order for the recommended, inspection or urgent job that belongs to the current latch stands in: raised after the latch began, or, while it awaits approval or is deferred, linked to the latch by a system escalation. An open instrument check, desk review or routine job never stands in for an inspection, and an older order already approved, scheduled or in progress is superseded: the inspection order is raised as well (PD-28; `tests/test_app_rereview_decision.py`).
  - New orders for the same latch come in three cases (PD-16, PD-18, PD-28):
    - a separate, higher-priority order when the condition becomes more urgent during lower-priority work in progress;
    - a new order after a rejection, once its suppression period ends (defaults 24 h for P1, 168 h for P2; `DecisionConfig.reject_suppress_h`), or at once if the condition escalates to a higher priority class. During a latch the suppression is keyed on the latch episode and on the jobs that can stand in for it, not on the displayed state: a scope or model flip does not re-raise a rejected inspection (`decision._suppressed_by_rejection`; `test_a_rejection_holds_for_the_latched_condition_whatever_the_displayed_state`). A rejection does not carry over to a new latch episode after a dismissal or a completed inspection (`test_a_rejection_does_not_suppress_a_new_latch_episode`);
    - a new order at the next assessment after an order standing in for the latch is completed without clearing it (outcome `not_inspected` or `other`, or completed by the integration role).
  - The last two happen whether or not the alarm persists.
  - So one latch can raise an inspection order every 7 days while each one is rejected, even after its alarm has stopped, and several episodes can share one order. Inspection orders per turbine-year are compared with W3's episodes per turbine-year only as an approximation.
- **Counting.** Orders are counted by `job_type`. An inspection recommendation can escalate, or be absorbed by, only an open order for the inspection or the urgent job (`decision._stand_in_jobs`). The wind type has no `repair` job, so with the shipped decision config an urgent condition raises an `inspect` order at P1 (`decision._plan_one`, `adapters/wind.py`) and only inspection orders stand in. Where a site's decision config names an urgent job the asset type has, an order for that job keeps its `job_type` when it stands in; the escalation shows as a `reprioritise` event whose reason ends `recommended job inspect` (`GET /api/v1/work-orders/{id}/history`). A recommendation absorbed by an open order (`open order covers it`) leaves no work-order record. Inspection recommendations are therefore counted from `job_type` plus those events, and cross-checked against the stored assessments (INSPECTION_RECOMMENDED, URGENT_REVIEW). An open equipment order also absorbs a desk-review recommendation (`open inspect order covers it`), so desk reviews are cross-checked against the assessments too.
- **Evidence (SYNTHETIC).** On W3's fresh held-out synthetic fleets 60–65, the shipped setting (margin 1.25, sustain 18 operating points; PD-15) gives **0.44 healthy alarm episodes per turbine-year**: 1 in 2.27 turbine-years (0.61 per assessable turbine-year), with a 95% interval up to about 2.45 (`product/evidence/wind_synthetic/w3.md`, `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md` E7).
  - That is below the proposed inspection ceiling of 2, on idealised data, but the interval does not exclude the ceiling.
  - The rate is sensitive to persistence: on W2's held-out fleets, sustain 6 (an earlier run's choice) gave 5.74 and sustain 12 gave 2.65 (`w2.md`).
  - Drifting temperature sensors also raise inspection orders, not instrument checks. They added 39 alarm episodes from 6 drifting sensors on the same fleets (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md` M1), and they count here whenever no drivetrain event follows.
  - **Under B3's own definition (every turbine without a drivetrain event), the held-out synthetic count is therefore (1 + 39) episodes in about 3.0 turbine-years (2.27 + 6 × 0.126), about 13 per turbine-year, above the ceiling.** That is an episode count, not an order count (Unit, above). It depends on the synthetic fleet having one drifting sensor per 10 turbines (`product/experiments/w2_threshold_revisit.py`, whose fleet design W3 reuses).
  - The real-data rate is unknown until G0 reports the product alarm count on normal datasets.
- **The ceiling of 2 may be too high.** A reviewer role-playing the head of O&M of a 200-turbine fleet judged it far too high: about 400 inspections a year (`ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md` om_manager-12). The ceiling is the partner's decision, made before the back-test.

## 4. Decision after the pilot

| outcome | consequence |
|---|---|
| all criteria pass | propose a rung-6 trial with defined actions, a pre-agreed baseline (e.g. matched turbines or a prior period) and a measurement plan for downtime and cost |
| B2 or S5 shows misses that matter, or S3/S4 fail | no rung-6 trial; publish the failure analysis; revisit the model or the vertical (`INITIAL_MARKET_AND_ASSET_CHOICE.md` §10) |
| S2 or S7 fails | fix the operational path and repeat the pilot |
