# Initial vertical and asset class: ACCESS-FIRST proposal

Research stream: vertical selection, access-first angle. Written 2026-10-18.

**Question.** Where could a small student team in India, with Python/ML skills, free tools, no special hardware and no customer access today, most plausibly get a **read-only shadow pilot with a real maintenance team within months**? Who would say yes, why, and through which door?

**Method and tool status.**
- WebSearch was **not available**. The shared per-turn budget of 200 searches was already used up when this stream started, so no search was run. WebFetch still worked, and was used only on primary URLs known in advance (no search engines, proxies or readers).
- Two PDFs (India Cooling Action Plan, AICTE Internship Policy) were saved by WebFetch, copied into an isolated scratch folder and converted to text locally with `pdftotext`. Page numbers below come from that text and may be off by one.
- Claims taken from the five earlier streams are tagged **[R1]** to **[R5]** with the URL those streams recorded. Those URLs were opened by the earlier streams on 2026-10-18 and were not re-opened here.
- **UNVERIFIED** marks anything not confirmed on a page opened in this session or by those streams. **Team judgement** marks reasoning that no source states.

**Data-type vocabulary** (same as R4): *real field* = measured on assets in normal service; *real laboratory* = measured on a test rig; *simulated* = output of a physics or system model; *synthetic* = generated by a statistical or rule-based process.

## 1. The proposal (one pair)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Vertical** | Indian higher-education campuses that maintain their own utilities through an in-house estate or works department, starting with the team's own institute |
| **Asset class** | Water-cooled central chilled-water plants: screw or centrifugal chillers plus their chilled-water pumps, condenser-water pumps and cooling-tower fans. Data come from plant-room log sheets, chiller-controller or BMS trend exports, and AMC (annual maintenance contract) service reports |
| **Geography** | India: the team's own campus first, then other campuses and hospitals reached through the same academic network |
| **Data in the pilot** | Real field data from day one, the first SentinelLoop has ever touched. It is not run-to-failure data |
| **Go/no-go gate (week 1)** | The campus has a central water-cooled plant with at least 2 chillers, and at least 12 months of log sheets or trend exports plus service records. If it does not, this proposal falls back to the next row in Section 9 |

**One-paragraph case (team judgement grounded in the sources cited in Sections 2 to 4).** The only door the team holds today is its own institution. A campus works department can say yes to a zero-cost, offline, read-only student project without buying anything, onboarding a vendor or connecting a network. AICTE's internship framework already gives credit for "consultancy/research project within the institutes", so the pilot has a recognised academic home [S5]. Of the utilities a campus maintains, a central chilled-water plant is the one most likely to produce regular multivariate condition readings without new hardware. It is rotating machinery with a known degradation process (heat-exchanger fouling, reset by cleaning). The Government of India's cooling plan names it as a priority for better operation and maintenance [S6]. And SentinelLoop's existing pattern (operating-regime normalisation, healthy-baseline index, degradation-to-threshold prognosis with a conformal interval, triage, audit) carries over to it directly.

## 3. Who would say yes, why, and through which door

### 3.1 Who
- **Owner of the asset and the yes:** the campus estate officer or works department engineer responsible for electrical and mechanical services. At IIT Kanpur, for example, the Institute Works Department page names a Superintending Engineer [S10]. That page does not say whether the department runs air-conditioning plants, so this is only an example of the organisational form.
- **Sponsor:** the Dean (infrastructure, planning or estates), Registrar or Director, who approves a student project touching institute assets (team judgement; titles vary by institute).
- **Academic anchor:** the team's faculty supervisor, ideally from refrigeration and air conditioning or thermal engineering. AICTE's policy gives faculty mentors a defined supervisory role [S5].
- **People affected:** chiller-plant shift operators, whose log sheets are the main data, and the AMC contractor's service engineer, whose visit reports are the work-order history. The contractor may see the pilot as an audit of its work, which is a risk (Section 8).

### 3.2 Why they would say yes

Sourced anchors:
- **The project fits an existing academic category.** AICTE's Internship Policy counts 14-20 credits of internship, project and similar work towards a B.Tech, at 40-45 hours per credit, or about 600-700 hours in total. One listed intra-institutional activity is working for a consultancy or research project within the institute [S5]. The policy's template letter to industry states that AICTE has made internship mandatory for all technical education students [S5]. The PDF's metadata dates it to November 2018, so whether a later revision supersedes it is UNVERIFIED.
- **Chiller O&M is a stated national priority.** The India Cooling Action Plan (MoEFCC, March 2019) says the chiller is the largest energy-consuming component of a central chilled-water system. It names better O&M practice, with training and certification of O&M professionals and more building automation, as a lever for existing chiller plants [S6, pp. 12, 28]. It also recommends using trained, certified technicians for air-conditioning maintenance in government ministries, departments and institutions [S6]. It counts educational institutions as one of eight commercial-building segments [S6].
- **Read-only by construction.** NIST SP 800-82r3's read-only patterns (DMZ, unidirectional gateways) are about network paths into control systems [R5]. An offline file handover creates no such path at all (team judgement).
- **Low safety stakes.** SentinelLoop is advisory, makes no SIL claim and must not be part of a safety function [R5]. A comfort-cooling plant is not a safety system.
- **Little personal data.** Technician identities in maintenance records are personal data under the DPDP Act 2023, and the asset owner is the Data Fiduciary [R5]. In this pilot the only personal data are operator and technician names, which can be pseudonymised at the source.

Hypotheses to confirm in the first meeting (UNVERIFIED):
- The estate team wants an independent, evidence-based check on AMC service quality and on chiller-plant electricity use.
- A student project costs the institute nothing and its staff only a few hours a month.
- The institute values visible student innovation on its own infrastructure.

### 3.3 Through which door
1. The faculty supervisor sponsors a one-page proposal. It states: read-only and offline; no connection to the BMS or chiller controllers; data stay on an institute-owned laptop; technician names pseudonymised; results go to the estate team first; anything published is anonymised and needs written consent.
2. The Dean, Registrar or Director approves it as an intra-institutional research project, which is creditable under AICTE's framework [S5].
3. The estate engineer nominates the plant in-charge as the day-to-day contact and authorises copies of log sheets, trend exports and AMC reports.

Contrast with external doors:
- **Tata Steel UK** qualifies vendors by invitation only, through SAP Ariba [R2].
- **CISA and 11 partner agencies** list 12 security elements that OT owners should require of products they buy [R2].
- **Indian central-government buyers** can relax EMD, prior-turnover and prior-experience rules only for DPIIT-recognised startups [R2].

None of these steps arises inside the team's own institute, because nothing is bought (team judgement).

## 4. Pilot access path and timeline

| Weeks | Step | Output | Read-only guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Proposal, approval, plant-room visit, go/no-go gate | Signed one-page data-use note; inventory of chillers, pumps, cooling-tower cells, tag list, log-sheet format, AMC report format | No access to any controller or network |
| 2-6 | Digitise 12 months of log sheets (double entry), import trend exports, code AMC reports as work orders in a local Odoo Community instance. The self-hosted Odoo `maintenance` module is LGPL-3 and exposes `maintenance.request` [R3][R5] | Back-test: regime baselines, health index, alerts the system would have raised, compared with recorded service actions | Copies only |
| 7-20 (one cooling season) | Shadow mode. Each week the team produces an advisory list (at most N items, N agreed with the plant in advance) without telling operators what to do. Operators and the AMC work as usual; afterwards the team records what they actually did | Agreement rate, false-alarm burden per week, missed events, plant-staff feedback, audit log of every advisory | Advisory list goes to the estate engineer only |
| 20+ | Exit review with the estate team | Written reference or permission for an anonymised case study; go or no-go for a second site | — |

**Ethics override (team judgement).** If an advisory points to a hazard, such as a refrigerant leak or electrical overheating, it goes to the plant in-charge the same day, outside the shadow protocol.

**Evaluation design (team judgement grounded in R1/R2).**
- **Pre-register the alert budget.** R1 reports alarm benchmarks of about one alarm per 10 minutes per console for large process plants; a weekly list is far below that. The real constraint is staff attention. R2 finds that early false alarms erode trust.
- **Pre-register the decisions the pilot informs.** R2's interview evidence favours decision-led over data-led design. Examples: when to schedule condenser-tube cleaning; which pump to inspect; whether an AMC visit fixed the flagged symptom.

## 6. Fit with the current system

| SentinelLoop today (simulated C-MAPSS) | Chilled-water plant pilot | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Per-cycle multivariate snapshots, 21 sensors, 3 operating settings | Hourly or per-shift snapshots of chiller temperatures, pressures, currents and loads | Good: same tabular snapshot shape (team judgement) |
| Operating-regime clustering and normalisation (D14) | Regimes defined by cooling load, number of chillers running and outdoor conditions | Good |
| Healthy-baseline health index (D10) | Baseline per chiller after each cleaning or service | Good |
| RUL to failure, capped at 125 cycles, with a conformal interval | Days until a health indicator (e.g. condenser approach temperature or kW per TR) crosses a plant-agreed threshold, with a conformal interval; cleaning acts as a reset and as censoring | Medium: needs a new target definition and handling of censoring |
| Age-matched drift monitor (D17) | Drift matched on season and load | Medium |
| Input-quality monitor, stress tests with injected faults | Manual-entry errors: transposed digits, copied-forward values, unit slips | Good, but new fault types are needed |
| Fleet rule across hundreds of engines | 2-4 chillers and 4-12 pumps or cooling-tower cells per plant | Weak: fleet statistics are thin |
| Deterministic triage rule table → draft order JSON | Weekly advisory list → `maintenance.request` in a local Odoo instance | Good |
| Hash-chained audit log | Audit of every advisory and its outcome | Good, after pseudonymisation |
| Gated retraining | Monthly re-fit on new weeks, with a gate | Medium: few new labels |

## 7. What must change in SentinelLoop

1. **Asset registry and time model.**
   - Plant, chiller, pump and cooling-tower records, with tags and units.
   - Real timestamps and irregular sampling instead of cycle indices. PRODUCT_STATUS lists both as missing.
2. **Ingestion.**
   - CSV/XLSX import for digitised log sheets and BMS trend exports, with a unit and range dictionary.
   - Double-entry reconciliation for hand-digitised values.
3. **Domain features.**
   - Condenser and evaporator approach temperatures, chilled-water delta-T, kW per TR, compressor current per unit of load, cooling-tower approach, and pump current against flow or head where logged.
   - These are standard chiller-performance quantities (team judgement; no source opened here lists them).
4. **Prognosis target.**
   - Degradation-to-threshold in days, with the threshold agreed with the plant.
   - Maintenance resets treated as events and censoring.
   - The 10/25/50-cycle severity thresholds and the 125-cycle cap are C-MAPSS conventions (TECHNICAL_DEBT A4) and must be replaced by plant lead times, such as the time needed to arrange an AMC visit.
5. **Drift monitor.** Re-key from engine age to season and load.
6. **Work-order side.**
   - Import AMC service reports as closed work orders.
   - Write advisories to a local Odoo Community instance only, never to the BMS. R5 documents the `maintenance.request` fields and the JSON-2 API.
7. **Persistence and model use.**
   - Stop re-runs from deleting stored decisions (TECHNICAL_DEBT B2).
   - Load the approved model version at inference instead of retraining (C1).
   - Without these two fixes, a season-long audit trail is impossible.
8. **Privacy.** Write only pseudonymous technician tokens into the hash chain, with the token-to-person map held separately and erasable by the institute. This is R5's proposed fix for the conflict with DPDP s.8(7) erasure; legal review is still needed [R5].
9. **Shadow-evaluation harness.**
   - Pre-registered metrics: agreement with actions taken, false alarms per week, missed events, lead time.
   - A weekly report format the estate engineer can read in five minutes.

## 8. Biggest risks

1. **The campus may have no central chilled-water plant.** ICAP says room air conditioners are about 80% of installed refrigerant-based cooling capacity in India. It names chiller systems as the preferred choice for large commercial buildings (hotels, hospitals, malls, office complexes, airports), and does not list educational buildings in that sentence [S6, pp. xiii-xiv, 12]. *Mitigation:* the week-1 gate. If it fails, try a larger campus or teaching hospital in the same network (hospitals are named in ICAP's list), or fall back to the next option in Section 9.
2. **Data quality.** Log sheets may be sparse, copied forward or partly illegible. R1 documents poor maintenance-data quality at nearly all industrial organisations, and miscoded fields in real work orders [R1]. Campus practice is UNVERIFIED.
3. **Few real events in one season.** Results will be case-study level (like MetroPT's 2-4 events [R4]), not accuracy statistics. RUL-to-failure cannot be validated this way, only degradation-to-threshold.
4. **Industrial credibility.** Comfort cooling on a campus is facilities maintenance, not plant maintenance. Per BSRIA figures quoted in ICAP, about 80% of chillers sold in India go to commercial buildings and about 20% to industrial air-conditioning applications [S6, p. 25]. A campus reference may carry little weight with plant buyers (team judgement).
5. **Competition.**
   - ICAP states that chiller analytics platforms offering fault detection and diagnostics are "readily available" [S6, p. 28].
   - Augury lists chillers among its assets, with Trane and Carrier among its OEM partners (vendor claim) [R3].
   - BMS and OEM vendors are likely competitors (UNVERIFIED).
   - The differentiation that remains is R3's unvalidated hypothesis: re-derivable audit, calibrated escalation, a vendor-neutral layer.
6. **Contractor resistance.** The AMC contractor may resist a tool that second-guesses its work. *Mitigation:* frame the pilot as support, share findings with the estate team only, and invite the contractor's engineer to review advisories.
7. **Seasonality.** Chiller run time depends on climate and the academic calendar. A pilot that misses the cooling season yields little.
8. **Scope creep away from the core model.** The C-MAPSS model family does not transfer. New physics features and a new target are needed (Section 7).
9. **Permission to publish.** The institute may refuse an external case study. Get consent terms in writing at the start.

But the team holds no MSME relationship today, and condition data usually need controller access or added hardware. | Large process plants (steel, cement), route-monitored pumps and motors | Discipline and data exist in TPM plants [R1]. But vendor qualification (Tata Steel UK Ariba, by invitation [R2]) and OT procurement security [R2][R5] gate access. Slow for an unincorporated student team |

| Turbofans (current data) | Engine makers sell health monitoring inside flight-hour risk-transfer contracts, which leaves no third-party route [R3] |
| Smart India Hackathon problem statements | Problem statements come from ministries, PSUs, industries and NGOs, but the SIH 2026 nomination deadline (09 Oct 2026) has passed. The landing page mentions no post-hackathon pilot or implementation support [S2]. Worth watching for SIH 2027 |
