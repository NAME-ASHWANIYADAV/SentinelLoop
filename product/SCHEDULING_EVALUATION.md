# Scheduling evaluation (Phase 4; corrected in Phase 9 as S2)

**Status: SIMULATED evidence only.** Every number below comes from a fleet simulator driven by out-of-fold predictions on NASA C-MAPSS engines (simulated turbofans). The costs are relative units chosen by us, inspection is assumed perfect, and the crews, windows and parts are invented. Nothing here is a measured saving, nothing here has been checked against a real maintenance organisation, and no schedule here is optimal for any real site.

Written 2026-10-18 (S1–S1e); updated the same day with S2 after the adversarial review (`ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md`: data_scientist-1, -2, -3, -5, -8, -9, -12, -14 and buyer-11). Code: `src/sentinelloop/app/scheduler.py` (scheduler and validator) and `src/sentinelloop/app/sim.py` (simulator).

- **Current evidence (S2):** `product/evidence/scheduling_v2/summary.md` and `partC_shipped.md`, generated by `product/experiments/s2_corrected.py` and `s2c_shipped_defaults.py` from the CSV files beside them.
- **History (S1–S1e):** `product/evidence/scheduling/`, kept in §6 with what S2 overturns marked.

## Conclusions (S2)

1. **Withdrawn: the MILP's downtime savings under parts shortage and adverse conditions.** They were a right-censoring artefact (data_scientist-1).
   - S1 counted an outage only when its corrective repair finished, so an asset still broken at day 120 counted nothing.
   - With that terminal downtime counted, the MILP and RISK_GREEDY have about the same total unplanned downtime: −201 h (−0.4%) under parts shortage and −82 h (−0.2%) under adverse conditions, out of about 54,000 and 46,000 h.
   - The counted-only figures had shown −60.7% and −57.5%.
   - The 0.0026-per-hour break-even downtime prices for these two scenarios are withdrawn with them.
2. **parts_shortage and adverse are fleet collapse. They are reported, not ranked** (data_scientist-2).
   - At day 120, 21.5–22.5 (parts_shortage) and 17.6–19.1 (adverse) of 30 assets are still failed, under every policy including run-to-failure.
   - Fleet availability is 0.367–0.373 and 0.461–0.485.
   - Once its failure is charged, a broken asset costs nothing more and cannot fail again, so a lower cost there does not mean a better policy.
   - The sustainable parts scenario is `parts_tight` (6 kits, 96 h lead time): 0.2–1.5 assets failed at the end, availability 0.934–0.980.
3. **No rule wins everywhere.** S2 tunes on FD001 engines and evaluates on disjoint FD003 engines (data_scientist-9). Each sustainable scenario has four result tables: two objectives, each tuned in base or in the scenario.
   - base, parts_tight and approval_delay: tuned RISK_GREEDY and tuned THRESHOLD_EDD are within 4.1 units in all four tables, and the per-seed differences span zero.
   - tight_labour: RISK_GREEDY is cheaper by 9.4–17.2 units, on 4–7 of 8 seeds.
   - degraded_predictions: THRESHOLD_EDD is cheaper by 8.9–22.1 units; the risk rule is cheaper on 0–1 of 8 seeds.
   - approval_delay: RISK_EDD (probabilistic trigger, earliest-due-date queue) is the cheapest policy in all four tables.
4. **The trigger comparison depends on the scheduler and on tuning.**
   - The claim (P17) that "with the same scheduler the risk trigger is never better on more than 2 of 8 seeds" is withdrawn (data_scientist-8).
   - With the EDD queue in S2, the risk trigger is cheaper on 6/8 seeds under approval delay (−11.8 units, tuned in base).
   - With the risk-greedy queue in S1e, it was cheaper on 8/8 seeds under tight labour.
   - The two triggers put their repairs in different priority classes. That confounds the risk-greedy, criticality-first and MILP contrasts, which read the class. The EDD list scheduler never reads it (`scheduler.py:213`), so RISK_EDD − THRESHOLD_EDD is not confounded by class; it still differs in due-date rule and in the risk trigger's inspections, which belong to the trigger itself (§4.4).
5. **S2 gives no support for fixed-interval as the safer default** (data_scientist-12).
   - The S1 statement that it is "the safer default when predictions fail" rested on the untuned threshold baseline.
   - Whether it is a safer fallback when predictions fail through noise, per-asset bias or drift was not tested (§5).
   - In S2 part B, fixed-interval is the most expensive policy other than run-to-failure in every sustainable scenario: 69–173 units above the tuned threshold rule, on 8/8 seeds. The same holds in the collapse scenarios, except that under parts shortage the base-tuned MILP costs more.
   - Under degraded predictions, the tuned threshold rule is the cheapest policy in all four tables.
   - That degradation is a uniform 30 h optimistic bias with wider intervals, which re-tuning cancels by construction. Noise, per-asset bias and drift were not simulated.
6. **The MILP keeps one advantage, under tight labour, and it is not a cost advantage at the price tried.**
   - It cuts total unplanned downtime by 4,663 h (−38%, lower on 8/8 seeds, hand-set parameters), and by 3,768 h on FD003 (−38%, 8/8, base-tuned).
   - It costs 24.1 and 22.5 units more when downtime is unpriced.
   - At 0.005 per downtime hour it is roughly break-even: paired differences −2.5 to +3.7 units, cheaper on 3–4 of 8 seeds. Break-even is 0.0044–0.0060 per hour.
   - It is never the cheapest policy in a part B table.
   - Its optimality holds only for its own 72-hour aggregated-capacity model (S1b), not for a plant.
7. **The platform ships the probabilistic trigger at the S1 placeholder settings** (data_scientist-3).
   - Code: the trigger is `states.py:112-115`. `configs/platform.yaml` sets p_inspect 0.10 and p_urgent 0.30, with a 60 h decision horizon for C-MAPSS. `risk_greedy` is the default planning policy (`api.py:151`).
   - These values were hand-set for S1. Tuning in base chose 60 h with 0.05/0.20 instead, in both S1d and S2.
   - Part C runs them on the S2 evaluation engines. They come within 2 units of base-tuned RISK_GREEDY in base, tight_labour and parts_tight. Under degraded predictions they cost 20.3 more (worse on 8/8 seeds), and 39.6 more than the tuned threshold rule. Under approval delay they cost 8.4 more (7/8).
   - Against RISK_GREEDY tuned in each scenario, which is what tuning on a site's own history would give, they cost 11.9 more under tight labour (8/8), 16.0 under approval delay (7/8) and 40.6 under degraded predictions (8/8) (§4.3).
   - The trigger relies on a P(failure) that is not calibrated (C2, PD-19).
   - A point-threshold RUL trigger is **PLANNED**, not implemented.
8. **The former default pair is withdrawn** (data_scientist-5). "Threshold trigger + `risk_greedy` queue" (PD-09) is the combination that collapses in S1e: +173.8 units over THRESHOLD_EDD under tight labour, better on 0/8 seeds. PD-27 supersedes PD-09.

**Product consequences** (PD-27 in `DECISION_LOG.md`, superseding PD-09):
- The platform keeps the probabilistic trigger with the `risk_greedy` queue at the placeholder settings. A site must tune them on its own history.
- A tuned point-threshold trigger with an EDD queue (THRESHOLD_EDD) is the comparison every site evaluation must include. It is PLANNED in the platform.
- Threshold trigger + `risk_greedy` queue is not recommended.
- The MILP stays a what-if planner and is never claimed optimal for a real site.
- Under fleet collapse, this evaluation reports the scenario without ranking schedules. Automatic scarcity reporting in the platform is **PLANNED** (PD-27 (e)). Today a schedule run stores each unscheduled job with its reason, such as "parts not available in time within the horizon" (`planning.py:69-76`, `scheduler.py:242-255`), and nothing flags a collapsing fleet.
- S2 gives no support for fixed-interval as a safer fallback: it is the most expensive policy other than run-to-failure in every simulated sustainable scenario. Its value as a fallback under noise, per-asset bias or drift was not tested.
- The downtime cost per hour is set per site: `downtime_cost_per_h` in the site decision file (PD-24). Without a file it is a placeholder 0, and `/api/v1/ready` says so.

**For the chosen vertical (wind, Phase 1):** the approved wind model is an anomaly model with no RUL and no failure probability (PD-11). None of the trigger results transfer: wind assessments produce inspections and urgent reviews with policy default due dates. Two things do transfer:
- the scheduler's behaviour under scarce labour;
- the need to price downtime. For a wind owner, downtime is lost generation, which is plainly not zero.

## 2. The scheduling problem as implemented

**Decision.** Which approved work orders start when, and with which technicians, over a rolling 72-hour horizon in 1-hour slots. The first 24 hours are committed; the remainder is re-planned daily.

**Hard constraints.** `scheduler.validate()` checks every schedule independently of the policy that produced it.
- A job needs `crew` technicians who hold its skill, are on shift and are free for every slot it occupies. A crew stays within one shift.
- A job starts and ends inside one maintenance window of its asset. A failed asset is available at any time.
- Only one job runs per asset at a time.
- Parts are used only once they are on hand: initial stock plus known deliveries. A part can be ordered only if it is marked orderable, and then arrives after its lead time.
- A job cannot start before its release time (approval delay).
- Frozen assignments (work already scheduled or in progress) are respected.

**Objective.** This is shared by every policy, so they can be compared. It is the expected loss of each job's start time:
- For a job with a prediction interval: failure cost × P(failure before the job ends).
- For a job without one: failure cost × floor[class] × hours to the job's end / horizon, where floor = {P1: 0.5, P2: 0.1, P3: 0.02}. This is a declared surrogate.
- For work left unscheduled: 1.5 × failure cost × max(P, floor).
- Optional stability term: a penalty proportional to floor × cost for moving a job that was in the previous plan.

**Policies.**

| policy | trigger | scheduler |
|---|---|---|
| RUN_TO_FAILURE | none (corrective only) | EDD |
| FIXED_INTERVAL | repair at a fixed age | EDD |
| THRESHOLD_EDD (the fixed-threshold baseline) | repair (P2) when point RUL ≤ threshold | EDD |
| RISK_CRITICALITY_FIRST (the criticality-first baseline) | P(failure before horizon) ≥ p_inspect → inspect (P2), ≥ p_urgent → repair (P1) | criticality A > B > C, then class, then due date |
| RISK_GREEDY / _STABLE | risk trigger | class, then expected loss avoided per crew-hour |
| RISK_EDD | risk trigger | EDD (S1e factorial; S2 parts A and B) |
| RISK_MILP / _STABLE | risk trigger | time-indexed MILP (HiGHS via `scipy.optimize.milp`), then exact technician assignment |
| THRESHOLD_CRITICALITY_FIRST, THRESHOLD_MILP, THRESHOLD_GREEDY | threshold trigger (S1e factorial only) | as named |

The priority classes come from `sim.py`:
- interval and threshold repairs are P2 (lines 218 and 226);
- urgent risk repairs are P1 (238);
- risk inspections are P2 (245), and a repair raised by an inspection that finds degradation is P1 (359);
- corrective work is P1 (335, 290).

## 3. Simulator and its assumptions

`sim.py`, every run:
- **Fleet.** 30 assets for 120 days, 3 h per C-MAPSS cycle (an assumption). Criticality mix 20/50/30% for A/B/C.
- **Resources.** Daily maintenance window 20:00–06:00. Four mechanics on two overlapping shifts. Eight repair kits with one-for-one reorder and a 72-hour lead time.
- **Jobs.** Inspection 2 h, one technician. Repair 8 h, two technicians. Corrective work starts 24 h after a failure.
- **Predictions.** Each asset runs through real out-of-fold predictions from the competition model, which never saw that engine. True failure is the engine's recorded end of life.
  - S1–S1e and S2 part A use FD001 + FD003 (200 engines).
  - S2 part B tunes on FD001 engines only and evaluates on FD003 engines only.
- **Inspection.** Assumed **perfect**: it finds degradation exactly when true RUL ≤ 40 cycles. This is optimistic for every policy that inspects.
- **Costs.** Failure: A 10, B 4, C 1.5. Planned repair 1. Inspection 0.1. **Downtime duration is not priced in "total cost".**
  - A failed asset does not age, so it cannot fail again (`sim.py:395-396`).
  - S2 reports total unplanned downtime, including assets still failed at the end.
  - S2 reports availability = 1 − (total unplanned + planned downtime) / fleet-hours.
  - S2 part B also tunes and reports a priced objective: total cost + 0.005 × total unplanned downtime hours.
- **Fairness.** All policies see the same fleet, trajectory order, starting ages and resources for a given seed and scenario.

| scenario | change from base | S2 status |
|---|---|---|
| tight_labour | 3 mechanics instead of 4 | sustainable |
| parts_tight | 6 kits, 96 h lead time (S2 only) | sustainable |
| parts_shortage | 2 kits, 168 h lead time | **COLLAPSE** |
| degraded_predictions | intervals 1.5× wider, all bounds 30 h optimistic | sustainable |
| approval_delay | every job released 24 h after creation | sustainable |
| adverse | tight labour + 3 kits at 168 h + degraded predictions + 12 h approval delay | **COLLAPSE** |

**Disclosed design history.**
- After early S1 runs, the base scenario's kit stock was raised to 8, because the first base setting was structurally short of kits. That was a scenario-design choice made after seeing results. The original shortage survives as the `parts_shortage` scenario.
- `parts_tight` was likewise added in S2, after the review showed that both scarcity scenarios collapse.

### 4.3 Part C: the settings the platform ships

The platform's RUL path uses the probabilistic trigger (`states.py:112-115`) at the `configs/platform.yaml` values: p_inspect 0.10, p_urgent 0.30, C-MAPSS decision horizon 60 h. Its default planning policy is `risk_greedy` (`api.py:151`).

### 4.4 Trigger, queue and scheduler: what S2 separates and what it does not

**Trigger: scheduler- and tuning-dependent.**
- **EDD queue (RISK_EDD − THRESHOLD_EDD, unpriced).**
  - Tuned in base: +9.3 (risk cheaper on 0/8) in base, +5.7 (3/8) tight_labour, +8.0 (1/8) parts_tight, +6.4 (1/8) degraded and **−11.8 (6/8) approval delay**.
  - Tuned in scenario: +9.3 (0/8), −1.2 (5/8), +4.6 (1/8), +43.5 (0/8) and −3.0 (5/8).
- **Risk-greedy queue.** Only S1e has this cell (THRESHOLD_GREEDY − RISK_GREEDY, both at S1d base-tuned settings). The risk trigger was better on 8/8 seeds under tight labour (+186.0), parts shortage (+45.9) and adverse (+83.4). The threshold trigger was better on 8/8 under degraded predictions (−24.8).
- **The confound, and where it applies.** Threshold repairs are P2 and urgent risk repairs are P1 (`sim.py:226-238`). The class matters only where the scheduler reads it.
  - `risk_greedy` ranks class first and criticality-first ranks it second (`scheduler.py:214-216`). Under `risk_greedy`, threshold repairs therefore queue behind every corrective job. The MILP uses the class in its unscheduled-work floor, its stability penalty and its assignment order (lines 132, 339, 412). The trigger contrasts under these three schedulers (S1 and S1e) are confounded by class, and no S1 or S2 cell holds it constant.
  - The EDD list scheduler sorts only by due date and job id (`scheduler.py:213`); corrective jobs carry no due date and sort last under either trigger. RISK_EDD − THRESHOLD_EDD is therefore not confounded by class. It still compares two triggers that differ in due-date rule (point RUL minus the repair time, against the time at which the model-implied P(failure) reaches 0.05 minus the repair time; `sim.py:227, 234`) and in the risk trigger's inspections. Both belong to the trigger itself. It is the cleanest trigger contrast S2 has: tuned in base, the risk trigger is cheaper on 0/8 seeds in base and on 6/8 under approval delay.

**Queue given the risk trigger (RISK_GREEDY − RISK_EDD).** Each family was tuned separately, and in every cell, in both tuning modes, the two families chose different trigger settings. For example, in base RISK_GREEDY chose 60 h, 0.05/0.2 and RISK_EDD 100 h, 0.10/0.30; under tight labour (unpriced, in scenario) 100 h, 0.05/0.2 against 150 h, 0.10/0.30. Every figure below therefore mixes the queue with trigger settings; none is a clean queue effect.
- **Unpriced, tuned in scenario:** −8.4 (greedy cheaper on 8/8) base, −12.1 (7/8) tight_labour, −6.0 (7/8) parts_tight, −34.6 (8/8) degraded, +2.1 (3/8) approval delay.
- **Unpriced, tuned in base:** the sign reverses under degraded predictions (+12.9, 0/8) and approval delay (+10.9, 2/8).
- **Priced, tight labour:** greedy stays cheaper with downtime priced at 0.005 per hour: −25.8 (7/8) tuned in base and −24.2 (7/8) in scenario.

**Criticality-first and the stable variants: re-run, not re-tuned.** Part A re-ran RISK_CRITICALITY_FIRST, RISK_GREEDY_STABLE and RISK_MILP_STABLE at the hand-set parameters in all seven scenarios; parts B and C did not run them.
- RISK_CRITICALITY_FIRST and RISK_GREEDY share the same trigger at the same settings in part A, so their difference is a queue effect.
- In `parts_tight`, RISK_CRITICALITY_FIRST had the lowest part A mean of all 9 policies: 144.7, against 145.1 for RISK_GREEDY, 145.6 for RISK_GREEDY_STABLE and 147.4 for RISK_MILP_STABLE. Paired against RISK_GREEDY it is −0.5 [−5.3, +5.8], cheaper on 4/8: a tie.
- In the other sustainable scenarios it cost 2.1–6.1 more than RISK_GREEDY, cheaper on 1–3 of 8 seeds.
- S1e's "+63 to +83 under scarcity" was THRESHOLD_CRITICALITY_FIRST − THRESHOLD_EDD in the two collapse scenarios. S2 did not run THRESHOLD_CRITICALITY_FIRST, and that figure is not a ranking.

## 5. What this evaluation does not establish

- **Anything about a real site.** Fleet, costs, crews, windows, perfect inspection and replenishment are assumptions.
- **Optimality of any schedule for a plant.**
  - The MILP is optimal only for its own aggregated model within its gap (S1b). S1 recorded 0 non-optimal plans in its 96 MILP runs, which S2 part A reproduces; the S2 `parts_tight` and part B MILP runs have no recorded solver status.
  - Its objective differs from the evaluation metric: the objective values restoring failed assets through the P1 floor; total cost ignores downtime.
- **The value of risk-based triggering for wind turbines.** The wind model gives no RUL and no probability.
- **Statistical significance beyond the stated seed-level paired counts.**
  - With 8 seeds, a "7/8 better" result is suggestive, not conclusive.
  - No engine-bootstrap intervals were computed.
  - FD003 is the only held-out engine set, and it has now been seen.
- **Robustness to prediction errors other than a uniform bias with wider intervals.** Noise, per-asset bias and drift were not simulated.
- **Any ranking under fleet collapse.** parts_shortage and adverse are not ranked.
- **That the platform makes these decisions.** THRESHOLD_EDD exists only in the simulator; the platform's point-threshold trigger is PLANNED.
- **Human behaviour:** approval latency beyond the fixed delays, overrides, and trust in the recommendations.
