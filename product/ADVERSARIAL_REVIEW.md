# Adversarial review (Phase 9)

**Generated** by `product/scripts/build_adversarial_review.py` from the workflow's structured output (`product/evidence/adversarial/review.json`) and the resolution map in that script.

## Method

- Six independent reviewers, each read-only, each required to cite file and line quotes or command output for every finding:
  1. head of O&M at an Indian wind IPP;
  2. senior reliability engineer / data scientist;
  3. IT/OT security assessor;
  4. site technician and shift planner;
  5. sceptical buyer (CFO) and investor;
  6. functional-safety and compliance auditor.
- Two adversarial verifiers then tried to refute every critical and high finding.
- The personas are role-played reviewers, not real customers, technicians or auditors. Their findings were checked against the code; their opinions about Indian O&M practice are labelled outside knowledge in the raw output.
- Medium and low findings were not adversarially verified.

**Findings:** 83 in total (4 critical, 30 high, 34 medium, 15 low).
**Verification of the 34 critical/high findings:** 14 confirmed, 20 partially_confirmed; severity after verification: 11 high, 23 medium.
**Resolution of all findings:** 23 DOCS, 41 FIXED, 6 OPEN, 13 PARTIAL.

## Bottom line from each reviewer

**Head of O&M, Indian wind IPP (~200 geared 2 MW turbines, Tamil Nadu and Gujarat, OEM service contracts on some sites).** The product team is honest about what it has not done: no interviews, no real turbine data, nothing above rung 2. The anomaly-only wind model refuses to invent probabilities. That honesty does not make it fit a wind O&M operation, and I would not let it near my sites yet. On my data path (daily OEM-portal exports), the documented deployment turns the whole fleet into DATA_QUALITY_FAILURE and raises instrument-check orders, because the staleness limit is 1 h and assessments default to "now". Idle, curtailed or freshly re-modelled turbines generate climbing inspection orders, and rejecting them only brings them back on the next run. The workflow logic has gaps that bear on safety: a technician can clear a latched URGENT gearbox alarm without any work order, and a deferral "until after monsoon" never comes back and drops out of every console view. Several things that drive Indian wind O&M are absent from both the data contract and the scheduler: curtailment, status and alarm codes, wind-speed limits for climbing, cranes, travel between turbines, multi-day jobs, multi-site crews, and OEM ticketing. On its own synthetic data the system is out of scope 35-59% of the time, so the proposed pilot would trip its own assessability stopping rule in the low-wind season.

**Senior reliability engineer / data scientist reviewing the evidence.** The evidence base is unusually reproducible and candid about its rung: my re-runs of the simulator matched the recorded numbers exactly, and the team overturned its own early 'risk wins' result. Several quantitative conclusions that drive product decisions do not survive scrutiny, though. The MILP's 56-61% downtime reduction under parts shortage and adverse conditions is an artefact of right-censoring: once the outages of assets still down at day 120 are counted, total downtime is identical. Both scarcity scenarios are fleet collapse (about 22 of 30 assets end failed), scored by a cost metric that rewards leaving assets broken. The product defaults in PD-09 are contradicted twice. The shipped code uses the probabilistic trigger at the untuned S1 settings, not the RUL threshold the documents describe. And the recommended 'threshold trigger + risk_greedy queue' is the exact combination that collapses in the team's own factorial (S1e). The P(failure) number shown to operators has never been calibration-checked and is badly miscalibrated on the team's own out-of-fold data (0.56 shown against 0.93 observed). On ideal synthetic data the wind model already produces 4-5 false-alarm episodes per turbine-year, more than twice the pilot ceiling of 2, and W1 hides this by reporting alarm-hours. The G0 pre-registration exists before any data are seen, which is good, but it leaves too many unspecified choices to be a binding falsification test.

**IT/OT security assessor for a power-sector customer.** The basics are sound. Every query is parameterised. API keys are 256-bit and stored only as hashes, and every non-health route needs a key. The audit key fails closed, and the CMMS connector defaults to shadow mode and enforces a host allowlist (47 targeted tests pass). The problems are in business-logic authorisation and in what the safety and integrity claims actually guarantee. With one call, a technician can clear a latched URGENT_REVIEW with no maintenance done. With one call, any holder of the 'assess' permission, including the integration machine key, can permanently stop new recommendations for an asset. The operator console listens on all interfaces over plain HTTP and asks for long-lived API keys, while DEPLOYMENT_GUIDE.md says it binds to localhost. As documented, the audit-verification procedure cannot detect truncation or direct edits of state tables, so 'tamper-evident audit of every platform action' is overclaimed. I would not approve a pilot until security-1 to security-4 are fixed and the THREAT_MODEL and SECURITY_REVIEW claims are corrected to match the code.

**Wind turbine site technician and shift planner (daily users of the operator console).** From the floor, the console is a thin, honest admin panel. It is not yet a tool a crew could run a shift from. Some things are right: it never makes up a failure probability, it asks for a written reason before a defer or reject, and the role gating works (10/10 console tests pass). But I ran it headlessly against a synthetic wind plant and found two ways a severe bearing alarm turns green with no inspection done. First, a technician can complete the order as "not_inspected" ("could not climb, wind above limit"). Second, a technician can type any reason into the Asset page's "Clear a latched warning" box. That breaks the latching promise in PD-08 and RELIABILITY_AND_SAFETY.md. There are also process dead ends. A deferred order disappears for good, a rejected one comes back an hour later, a technician who could not do a job has no way to hand it back, and nobody can raise the follow-up component job the wind adapter says a person must choose. The technician's job card does not say which component or sensor is involved, the wording is raw codes and ML terms, and times are shown in UTC. On top of that, the console needs a live VPN connection and a 47-character key. Fix the latch-clearing paths and the defer/reject loop before any pilot. Even a shadow pilot will teach crews to ignore or work around the tool if these stay.

**Sceptical buyer (CFO of an Indian onshore-wind IPP) and investor doing diligence.** I would not sign a purchase order or a term sheet on this pack. That is mostly because the business evidence does not exist yet, and the documents mostly say so: no customer has been contacted, no real turbine data has been run, business value sits on rung 0, and savings are explicitly out of scope. The honesty is real. Negative results are kept, vendor claims are labelled, and the numbers I spot-checked in the matrix (P3, P6, P17, P18) match their evidence files. Diligence problems remain, though. The README front door still makes the competition-era claims ("Self-Healing Machines", "calibrated 90% interval") that the product rules forbid. The canonical claim matrix, validation ladder, requirements and architecture have not been updated since Phases 2–4, so they contradict the code and the integration guide. The matrix credits the product with retraining and drift monitoring that exist only in the frozen competition code. And the stated value proposition for the wind vertical ("calibrated early warning", a "ranked list ... under a crane budget ... with stated risk") describes things the shipped code does not compute. There is no pricing hypothesis, no ROI method, no commercial pilot terms and no reference of any kind, and the known-limitations register never lists those business gaps. Reviewed at HEAD b3248ec (the branch moved during the review). Uncommitted source edits by another party were present and were not relied on.

**Functional-safety and compliance auditor.** The advisory boundary holds at the code level. Nothing in the platform can reach a control system. A system recommendation cannot be scheduled, sent or executed until a human approver acts, and the compliance language is unusually restrained: there are no IEC 61508, IEC 62443, ISO 55000, CEA or DPDP claims. The weak point is the central safety guarantee, latching and fail-visible states (PD-08, RELIABILITY_AND_SAFETY §1.3–1.4). Ordinary workflow steps defeat it, and I reproduced each case with scratch scripts: completing any work order on an asset clears a latched URGENT_REVIEW, even an unrelated job, a 'not_inspected' outcome, or an order started before the urgent assessment arrived. A technician can clear URGENT with free text and no work order. Two back-dated assessments clear a live DATA_QUALITY_FAILURE. A request already sent to the CMMS is never withdrawn when SentinelLoop cancels or defers the order. Several 'tested' claims in the safety documents are broader than the tests behind them, and the threat model a partner would review predates the CMMS write path. Fix these before a partner's safety function signs precondition P5 of the pilot protocol.

## What the review changed in the product's position
